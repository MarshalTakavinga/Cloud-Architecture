# ADR-029: Cloud Platform Selection — Azure

**Status:** Approved
**Date:** Step 10 of the Case Study 2 pipeline

## Context

[Steps 6](../docs/azure-implementation.md)–[9](../docs/private-cloud-implementation.md) built four independent, equally rigorous implementations of the same [Step 5](../docs/logical-design.md) logical design on Azure, AWS, GCP, and private cloud. [`docs/decision-matrix.md`](../docs/decision-matrix.md) scores all four against `requirements.md`'s own priority weighting. This ADR records the resulting platform decision and the reasoning behind each score, at ADR depth.

## Decision

Palisade's new real-time payments capability is built on **Microsoft Azure**, scoring 4.25/5.00 (85.0%) against the weighted decision matrix — the highest of the four tracks, though not by a wide margin over AWS (4.10/5.00, 82.0%).

## Scoring Rationale, Criterion by Criterion

1. **Regulatory/resiliency fit (25%, highest weight) — Azure: 5, AWS: 4, GCP: 3.5, Private cloud: 3.** Azure's [ADR-006](ADR-006-azure-ledger-of-intent-database.md) SQL Ledger feature is the only database-native, cryptographically verifiable immutability guarantee among all four tracks — a direct, examiner-legible answer to "prove this record wasn't altered." AWS's S3 Object Lock ([ADR-012](ADR-012-aws-ledger-of-intent-database.md)) and GCP's Cloud Storage Bucket Lock ([ADR-018](ADR-018-gcp-ledger-of-intent-database.md)) are real but weaker, object-storage-level WORM controls, following Amazon QLDB's 2024 discontinuation. Private cloud's audit immutability ([ADR-025](ADR-025-private-cloud-database.md)) is application-enforced only (an INSERT-only role and checksums), the weakest of the four, and its RTO/RPO story carries no cloud SLA behind it.
2. **Real-time payments delivery within 18 months (20%) — AWS: 4.5, Azure: 4, GCP: 3.5, Private cloud: 2.5.** AWS's [ADR-016](ADR-016-aws-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md) enrollment approach for the 2021 account requires no rebuild of those legacy workloads, a genuine schedule advantage the Azure and GCP tracks don't share — both require a full cross-cloud replatform of that footprint ([ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md), [ADR-022](ADR-022-gcp-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md)). Private cloud's hardware procurement and installation lead time ([ADR-023](ADR-023-private-cloud-platform-and-facility-strategy.md)) is the largest schedule risk of the four against a hard, board-committed deadline.
3. **Fraud-detection latency fit (15%) — Private cloud: 5, all three hyperscalers: 4.** [ADR-026](ADR-026-private-cloud-network-topology.md)'s same-facility placement eliminates the WAN hop every hyperscaler track's hybrid-connectivity circuit carries ([ADR-008](ADR-008-hybrid-connectivity.md), [ADR-014](ADR-014-aws-hybrid-connectivity.md), [ADR-020](ADR-020-gcp-hybrid-connectivity.md)) — a genuine structural latency advantage. All four tracks independently chose a compute platform with warm minimum-instance counts specifically to protect NFR-4's budget, so the hyperscalers are tied.
4. **Cost trajectory impact (15%) — GCP: 4.5, all others: 3.5.** [ADR-019](ADR-019-gcp-messaging.md)'s single-product Pub/Sub answer avoids both Azure's Premium-tier Service Bus cost ([ADR-007](ADR-007-azure-messaging.md), accepted there specifically because ordering correctness on a real-money flow justified it) and AWS's two-product SNS+SQS FIFO spend ([ADR-013](ADR-013-aws-messaging.md)). Private cloud's capex-vs-opex trade-off ([ADR-023](ADR-023-private-cloud-platform-and-facility-strategy.md)) cuts both ways and nets out even with Azure and AWS here, not ahead.
5. **Operational/skills fit (15%) — Azure, AWS, GCP: 4.5 (tied), Private cloud: 2.5.** All three hyperscaler tracks give Palisade a fully managed, cluster-free container platform (Container Apps, Fargate, Cloud Run), directly addressing the cloud-skills-gap constraint. Private cloud is the clear outlier: [ADR-024](ADR-024-private-cloud-compute-platform.md) named plainly that this track has no escape from operating a Kubernetes control plane, and [ADR-025](ADR-025-private-cloud-database.md), [ADR-027](ADR-027-private-cloud-identity.md), and [ADR-028](ADR-028-private-cloud-messaging.md) each carry the same recurring finding at the database, identity, and messaging layers.
6. **Data residency and portability (10%) — Private cloud: 5, all three hyperscalers: 4 (tied).** Full data and infrastructure ownership gives private cloud the strongest portability and residency story by construction — no platform-specific service to migrate away from later. All three hyperscaler tracks satisfy NFR-6 equally (single US region), so they tie below it.

## Alternatives Considered (rejected, retained here rather than deleted)

1. **AWS.** A close second (82.0% vs. Azure's 85.0%), and the strongest track on the single most schedule-critical criterion. Rejected only narrowly — the regulatory/resiliency gap (no database-native cryptographic ledger) is the deciding factor given that criterion's 25% weight, the highest of any criterion in this matrix.
2. **GCP.** Rejected — carries the same regulatory/resiliency gap as AWS without AWS's compensating schedule advantage on the 2021-account question, and scores lowest of the three hyperscalers on the highest-weighted criterion.
3. **Private cloud.** Rejected — while it has the strongest fraud-latency and data-portability story of the four, it scores lowest on both of the two highest-weighted criteria (regulatory/resiliency and 18-month delivery) for reasons named plainly across [ADR-024](ADR-024-private-cloud-compute-platform.md) through [ADR-028](ADR-028-private-cloud-messaging.md) — an unavoidable Kubernetes-operations burden, a weaker audit-immutability guarantee, and real hardware-procurement schedule risk against a board-committed deadline.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** The platform decision is driven by the requirement `requirements.md` itself names as highest priority (regulatory/resiliency fit) rather than by cost or convenience, consistent with driver 2's standing as a non-negotiable compliance bar.
- **Positive:** Azure's own track ([ADR-005](ADR-005-azure-compute-platform.md)–[ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md)) is already fully specified at ADR depth — nothing further needs to be designed from scratch before [Step 11](../docs/target-architecture.md) restates it as the confirmed target.
- **Negative / accepted trade-off:** Azure does not offer AWS's schedule advantage on the 2021 account, or GCP's messaging cost advantage, or private cloud's latency and portability advantages — each of those genuine strengths is named here rather than erased by the overall result, and none of the three runner-up platforms is a poor choice on its own terms.
- **Carried to Step 11:** Restating the confirmed Azure target architecture and tracing each forcing function to the specific mechanism that closes it.
- **Carried to Step 12:** The 2021 AWS account workloads' migration into Azure's second landing-zone spoke ([ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md)) is a concrete migration-roadmap item, now confirmed as in-scope since Azure was selected.
