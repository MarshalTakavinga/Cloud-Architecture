# ADR-016: AWS Multi-Account Landing Zone and the 2021 Account's Disposition

**Status:** Approved
**Date:** Step 7 of the Case Study 2 pipeline

## Context

`current-state.md` documents the 2021 AWS account as having no landing zone, no network segmentation, no centralized identity federation, and no documented data-classification review, and `requirements.md` carries that account's governance gap forward as a named risk to be resolved by whatever platform this case study builds, not worked around. This decision is materially different from its Azure counterpart ([ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md)): on Azure, the 2021 workloads are a *destination* problem (where do they get replatformed to). On AWS, that account is *already a resource of the exact platform being evaluated* — the only one of the four tracks in this case study where that is true. This ADR has to decide both the new landing zone's shape and, specifically, what happens to that pre-existing account.

## Decision

Palisade stands up a new **AWS Organization managed by AWS Control Tower**, using account-level isolation — AWS's own strongest blast-radius boundary — rather than importing Azure's single-subscription hub-spoke shape. A dedicated **Payments account** holds the new real-time payment-processing workloads (Fargate, Aurora, SNS/SQS, and the Direct Connect attachment from [ADR-014](ADR-014-aws-hybrid-connectivity.md)), alongside Control Tower's standard foundational accounts (Security/Audit, Log Archive). The existing 2021 AWS account is **enrolled as a member account into this new Organization**, under a separate **Legacy-Digital-Channels OU** — not the Payments OU — after an access and configuration audit (IAM users, security groups, resource inventory). Enrollment, not rebuild, is the default: Control Tower can bring an existing account under organization-wide guardrails without requiring its workloads to be recreated from scratch. Each account gets its own VPC; only the Payments VPC connects to Palisade's data center. Service Control Policies (region restriction to US regions per NFR-6, deny-public-resource guardrails), an AWS Config conformance pack, Security Hub, and GuardDuty are enabled organization-wide from the Security/Audit account.

## Alternatives Considered (rejected, retained here rather than deleted)

1. **A single-account landing zone**, with the new payment-processing workloads and the 2021 account's workloads segmented only by VPC, mirroring Azure's hub-spoke-in-one-subscription shape. Rejected — a platform-native AWS answer should use AWS's own strongest isolation primitive (the account boundary — IAM policy mistakes, service quotas, and billing are all scoped per account) rather than import a shape designed around Azure's subscription/VNet model. Mixing an OCC-scrutinized payment-processing workload with lower-sensitivity legacy notification/analytics workloads in one account, even with VPC segmentation, is a weaker structural boundary than the account-level separation AWS's own guidance recommends for exactly this kind of risk-tiering.
2. **Leave the 2021 AWS account exactly where it is, outside the new Organization, governed only by policy on paper.** Rejected outright — this is the same governance-gap-left-unresolved failure mode [ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md) rejected for Azure, and `requirements.md` is explicit that this gap must be resolved as part of the new landing zone, not carried forward unaddressed.
3. **Decommission the 2021 account entirely and rebuild its workloads from zero** inside the new Organization. Rejected as the default plan — Step 4's own 6-R disposition already classified these workloads as **Replatform**, not Rebuild/re-architect; `current-state.md` names the account's *governance*, not its workloads, as the problem. A full rebuild would solve a problem current-state review never actually identified, at needless cost and schedule risk. A rebuild remains a fallback only if the enrollment audit turns up something disqualifying — not the first move.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** This closes the exact governance gap `requirements.md` names, using AWS's own native multi-account primitive rather than a borrowed single-account shape — and does it by enrolling the existing account rather than rebuilding it, which is directly responsive to Step 4's Replatform disposition rather than silently overriding it.
- **Positive:** The Payments account's blast radius (IAM, service quotas, billing, security findings) is structurally isolated from the legacy account by the account boundary itself, not just a VPC/subnet boundary — a stronger isolation guarantee than Azure's equivalent hub-spoke design offers within a single subscription.
- **Negative / accepted trade-off:** Multi-account AWS Organizations governance (Control Tower, SCPs, cross-account logging and observability) is materially more setup surface than a single-subscription hub-spoke VNet — accepted deliberately, for the same reason [ADR-010](ADR-010-azure-landing-zone-and-segmentation.md) accepted the equivalent Azure setup cost, and arguably a larger lift here specifically because a live account with real, imperfectly documented workloads has to be audited and enrolled, not designed on a blank slate.
- **Carried to Step 12:** The 2021 account's enrollment audit (IAM users, security groups, resource inventory, and any undocumented dependencies `requirements.md` flags as a risk) is a concrete migration-roadmap action item, recorded here so Step 12 does not have to rediscover it.
- **Carried to Step 13:** Control Tower foundational-account costs (Log Archive, Security/Audit) are a sizing detail deferred to Step 13.
