### ADR-037: Decoupling cyber-insurance compliance conditions from the full migration timeline

**Context:**
`requirements.md` names an explicit risk: "A rushed, compliance-driven timeline (cyber-insurance renewal) could push toward a platform choice that isn't fully evaluated." That risk to the *platform decision* was already mitigated by running the full Step 10 decision matrix before committing to anything (ADR-034), even under time pressure. But the underlying schedule pressure is real and separate from which platform was chosen: the insurer's renewal conditions — organization-wide MFA, encryption at rest with no exceptions, immutable/geographically-separate backups, and annually tested DR (`current-state.md` §7) — are conditions on the *organization*, and the full 46-site migration to the approved Azure target architecture runs on the order of a year (`migration-roadmap.md`). Waiting for full migration completion before satisfying any of the four conditions means carrying today's already-realized-incident risk profile — no MFA enforcement, unencrypted legacy LUNs, tape/courier backup, tabletop-only DR testing — for the entire length of the program.

**Options considered:**
- Wait until full migration completion (all 46 sites cut over) to satisfy all four insurer conditions together
- Treat each of the four conditions as its own workstream, satisfied against whichever environment — legacy on-prem or the new Azure landing zone — can deliver it fastest, independent of the site-by-site compute wave schedule
- Ask the insurer for a conditional/phased renewal tied to the migration roadmap's milestones, without making any near-term technical change

**Decision:** Treat each of the four insurer conditions as an independent workstream, sequenced into Wave 0 and Wave 1 of `migration-roadmap.md` rather than waiting for the full program to complete.

**Rationale, condition by condition:**
- **Organization-wide MFA**: Entra Connect (hybrid sync from the existing on-prem AD forest) plus Entra ID P2 Conditional Access is already part of the approved target identity design (`azure-implementation.md` §5, ADR-009's own dependency on it). Deploying it in Wave 0, together with the NPS Extension for Azure MFA in front of the *existing* on-prem VPN/Citrix authentication path, enforces MFA for every clinical and administrative sign-in within weeks — without waiting for the Citrix compute tier itself to migrate. This is not throwaway work: it's the identical hybrid-identity foundation the finished Azure design needs anyway, just built and turned on first.
- **Encryption at rest, no exceptions**: the specific gap named in `requirements.md` is the legacy unencrypted LUNs, including some patient-imaging archive volumes (`current-state.md` §5). That is a local, on-prem SAN-configuration fix — enabling the Dell EMC Unity SAN's native at-rest encryption on the remaining unencrypted volumes — with zero dependency on the cloud migration. Done in Wave 0.
- **Immutable, geographically separate backups**: rather than waiting for workloads to move before the backup story improves, the existing Veeam backup jobs are pointed at a new immutable Azure Blob Storage target (retention-lock policies, geographically separate from Independence, OH by construction) in Wave 0 — for the *current* on-prem data, not just future Azure-hosted data. This is also not throwaway: it's the same immutable-backup pattern the target design already uses, started early against the legacy environment instead of only the new one.
- **Annually tested DR**: this condition genuinely cannot be front-loaded the same way — a real tested failover needs an actual target environment to fail over to. But it does not need to wait for the *full* 46-site program either: once Wave 1's central-services cutover (database, integration bus, portal, telehealth) is live and stable, a live DR failover test against the paired Azure region becomes possible — targeted shortly after Wave 1's bake period closes, not deferred to the program's final wave. This gives the insurer a genuine live-tested result many months earlier than waiting for every clinic's compute tier to finish migrating.

**Trade-off:**
The on-prem SAN encryption work in Wave 0 is genuinely throwaway once those workloads' data fully moves to Azure and the legacy LUNs are decommissioned — but it is cheap, fast, and closes a named compliance gap for as long as that data still lives on-prem, which is most of the program's ~14-month duration, so the trade-off is accepted deliberately rather than skipped as wasted effort. The MFA and immutable-backup workstreams carry no such waste, since they're the same target-state components, just sequenced earlier. This ADR does not change or accelerate the underlying migration wave schedule (ADR-035/ADR-036) to chase compliance faster — the insurer conditions are satisfied by decoupling them from the wave schedule, not by compressing it.

**Status:** Approved
