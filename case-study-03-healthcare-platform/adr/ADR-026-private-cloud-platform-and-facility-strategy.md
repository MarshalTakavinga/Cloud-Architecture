### ADR-026: Private-cloud platform software and physical facility strategy

**Context:**
This ADR has no equivalent in `azure-implementation.md`, `aws-implementation.md`, or `gcp-implementation.md` — and that absence is itself the point. "Azure," "AWS," and "GCP" are each a single vendor-neutral thing to implement *against*: the facility, the physical hardware, the power and cooling redundancy, and the choice of virtualization/networking software are all already decided, invisible, and included the moment a region is picked. "Private cloud" is not a single thing. Before any of the seven ADRs that follow (ADR-027 through ADR-033) can decide how to host CareLink PM, the database, the network, or anything else, two coupled questions specific to this track have to be answered: what software platform actually turns physical servers into a self-service private cloud, and where does that hardware physically live. `current-state.md` already shows what happens when the second question is answered badly — a single converted server room at HQ, single utility feed, one aging generator, single-carrier fiber, caused a 14-hour outage in January 2025.

**Options considered — platform software:**
- VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF) — ESXi hypervisor, vSAN software-defined storage, NSX software-defined networking, vCenter/SDDC Manager/Aria Suite for automation and self-service, organized into vCenter-scoped Workload Domains
- OpenStack — the leading open-source private-cloud platform (Nova/Cinder/Neutron/Keystone mapping to compute/storage/network/identity)
- Nutanix — an HCI-first private-cloud platform and VCF's closest commercial competitor

**Options considered — physical facility:**
- Rebuild and harden the existing Independence, OH HQ server room (redundant utility feeds, a second generator, carrier-diverse fiber)
- Commercial colocation: lease space and power in one or more existing multi-tenant data center facilities with redundant power/cooling/network already built and contractually guaranteed
- Hybrid: primary production in a hardened HQ facility, DR in colocation, or the reverse

**Decision:** VMware Cloud Foundation, deployed across two commercial colocation facilities in geographically separate metro areas — a primary production facility in the Columbus, OH metro and a secondary/DR facility in the Dallas, TX metro — with no production workload retained in the HQ server room.

**Rationale:**
Rebuilding in place at HQ is rejected outright, not weighed as a cost trade-off: it is the exact single-facility, single-utility-feed, single-generator design that caused the January 2025 outage (`current-state.md` §7), and "private cloud" cannot honestly mean "the same converted server room, patched." This is the single most important framing decision in this whole track, stated explicitly so it isn't lost in the sizing tables that follow: choosing private cloud does **not** mean choosing to do everything Meridian's own 16-person team has never done before — it means choosing to own the virtualization/software layer while still *buying* the physical facility as a service, the same way the reference guide frames colocation as a legitimate point on the spectrum between "our own building" and a hyperscaler region, not a compromise choice. Two geographically separate commercial colocation facilities make redundant utility feeds, generator capacity, carrier diversity, and physical security a contractual SLA the colocation provider owns, not something a 16-person team designs, builds, and maintains from scratch — directly removing the root cause of the January 2025 incident rather than insuring around it.

On platform software: OpenStack is rejected on staffing grounds — the identical objection ADR-002 raised generically against full microservices and ADR-019/ADR-022 raised against GKE/AKS/EKS for this same team. Nova/Cinder/Neutron/Keystone demand deep in-house distributed-systems operating expertise this team does not have today, and OpenStack would be a green-field platform with no bridge from what the team already runs. Nutanix is rejected for a narrower, more specific reason: Meridian is not buying a private cloud from scratch. `current-state.md` §3 shows a live VMware vSphere 6.7 estate (six Dell PowerEdge R740 hosts) and, implicitly, existing VMware-administration skill on the current 16-person team. VCF is the direct upgrade path from that estate and those skills; Nutanix would be a full platform replacement — new hypervisor, new storage layer, new admin tooling, new training — for no corresponding capability gain Nutanix offers that VCF doesn't. VCF is chosen as the smallest real change from what Meridian's team already knows how to run, the same "smallest change that satisfies the strategy" logic ADR-019 applied when it kept Citrix rather than switching VDI platforms.

**Trade-off:**
This is the trade-off that every subsequent ADR in this track inherits, and it should be named with full weight here rather than softened. Private cloud carries real capital commitment (hardware is purchased and owned, not rented; colocation contracts typically run multi-year terms) and Meridian's own team now owns patching, capacity planning, and hardware lifecycle for the entire stack below the guest VM layer — the exact category of operational burden ADR-001 and ADR-003 were trying to reduce for CareLink PM's database, still fully present here at the infrastructure layer, just relocated to a better facility. **A second, structural trade-off worth naming now because it shapes every DR decision in this track**: a hyperscaler's "warm standby, scale up on failover" pattern (ADR-004) is an API call against effectively unlimited capacity. A private-cloud DR facility's scale-up is bounded by whatever physical hardware is already racked, powered, and licensed there — hardware cannot be procured, shipped, and installed in the middle of a declared disaster. This design accepts that constraint by provisioning the DR facility to match full target production capacity from day one (see Proposed Configuration), not a true elastic pilot light — a real, ongoing capital cost the hyperscaler tracks do not carry, flagged here for heavy weight in Step 10 and Step 13, not discovered later as a surprise.

**Status:** Proposed

**Proposed Configuration:**

| Setting | Proposed value | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform software | VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF), current release train | Direct upgrade path from the existing vSphere 6.7 estate and staff skills — see Rationale |
| Primary facility | Tier III+ commercial colocation, Columbus, OH metro | Close enough to Independence, OH HQ for practical hands-on operations and hardware logistics; established, carrier-dense data center market with multiple redundant utility/fiber providers to choose among |
| Secondary/DR facility | Tier III+ commercial colocation, Dallas, TX metro | Geographically separate region from Columbus (different power grid interconnection, different weather-risk profile, different carrier paths) — the private-cloud analog to the `us-central1`/`us-west1` region pair used across the Azure/AWS/GCP tracks |
| Facility redundancy per site | Dual utility feeds, N+1 generator, dual-carrier diverse fiber entry, biometric physical access control, provided contractually by the colocation operator | Directly closes the single-utility-feed/single-generator/single-carrier gap named in `current-state.md` §3/§7 |
| Management Workload Domain (per facility) | 4 ESXi hosts minimum (VCF's standard N+1 management-domain floor), dual-socket, 16–24 cores/socket, 512 GB RAM | Hosts vCenter Server, NSX Manager cluster, SDDC Manager, and the Aria Suite (Automation, Operations, Log Insight) — the platform's own control plane, sized for resilience independent of production workload growth |
| Production VI Workload Domain — primary facility, day one | 8 ESXi hosts, dual-socket, 32 cores/socket (64 cores/host), 1 TB RAM, all-NVMe vSAN | Sized to day-one CareLink PM/SQL/Tanzu load — see ADR-027/ADR-028/ADR-030 for the workload math this host count is built from |
| Production VI Workload Domain — primary facility, 36-month target | Scales to ~20 ESXi hosts | Sized to the same 5,000-concurrent-session 36-month planning ceiling used across every platform track (`requirements.md`) |
| Production VI Workload Domain — DR facility | Provisioned to match full 36-month target capacity (~20 hosts) from day one, not scaled incrementally | Direct consequence of the Trade-off above: physical hardware cannot be procured mid-incident, so the DR facility cannot be a true elastic pilot light the way the hyperscaler DR regions are — this is a deliberate, named cost, not an oversight |
| Hardware refresh cadence | 5-year lifecycle, staggered by Workload Domain | Matches typical enterprise server/vSAN hardware warranty and support windows; avoids the "average age 6.5 years, past vendor end-of-life" state `current-state.md` §3 describes today |
| Inter-facility connectivity | Redundant, carrier-diverse private circuits (see ADR-029) between Columbus and Dallas facilities | Required for synchronous/asynchronous replication (ADR-028) and NSX network extension (ADR-029) — this is Meridian's own contracted circuit, unlike a hyperscaler's included inter-region backbone |

Hardware, licensing (VCF subscription/perpetual terms), and colocation contract costs are deliberately not estimated here — Step 13 by design, the same discipline every other platform ADR in this case study applies. This ADR fixes the platform and facility strategy every subsequent private-cloud ADR builds on; see `docs/private-cloud-implementation.md` §1 for how this maps onto the full logical-component service mapping.
