### ADR-031: Identity provider for patient-facing authentication in the private-cloud design

**Context:**
ADR-002's Zero Trust decision assumed a single identity provider verifying every call. That holds for staff and providers: on this platform, they authenticate against Meridian's existing on-prem Active Directory forest directly — VCF-managed VMs simply domain-join it, since there's no cloud tenancy boundary between the private-cloud facilities and the AD forest already running at HQ the way there is on Azure/AWS/GCP. **This is a genuine private-cloud simplification worth naming**: ADR-009 (Azure), ADR-016 (AWS), and ADR-023 (GCP) each needed a hybrid-identity extension product (Entra Connect, AWS Directory Service, Google Cloud Managed Service for Microsoft AD) to bridge a cloud tenancy back to the on-prem forest. Here there is no bridge to build — the workforce identity story is simply "the same AD forest, reached from a new location." Patients are a different problem, and the same one every platform faces: they have no corporate directory account, and putting them in one would be both wrong and a security problem, the identical framing ADR-009/ADR-016/ADR-023 used.

**Options considered:**
- Extend the on-prem Active Directory forest to also hold patient accounts
- Self-host an open-source CIAM (customer identity and access management) product — e.g., Keycloak — on the Tanzu platform (ADR-030/ADR-032)
- A managed SaaS CIAM product (e.g., Okta Customer Identity Cloud, Auth0), consumed over the internet regardless of where the rest of the platform lives

**Decision:** A managed SaaS CIAM product, kept as a distinct identity population from workforce Active Directory.

**Rationale:**
Extending the workforce AD forest to also hold patient accounts is rejected for the identical reason ADR-009/ADR-016/ADR-023 rejected it everywhere else: it mixes a consumer population with materially different risk and lifecycle characteristics into the directory that governs access to clinical systems. The real decision unique to this track is self-hosted-open-source versus SaaS, and **this is where the "keep operational burden in-house" theme running through ADR-027 through ADR-030 deliberately reverses.** A self-hosted Keycloak cluster on Tanzu would mean Meridian's own team owning the uptime, patching, and security posture of the single most attack-sensitive component in the entire platform — the system holding ~2 million patients' credentials, for an organization that just absorbed a real credential-compromise incident (`current-state.md`, March 2026). That is exactly the kind of workload the reference guide's own workload-placement framework argues should not be forced on-premises purely because the rest of the platform is private cloud — "private cloud" as a Step 10 option does not have to mean "100% self-hosted, nothing SaaS," any more than the Telehealth Repurchase decision (ADR-002) meant "100% self-built." A managed CIAM SaaS product gets Meridian MFA, breach-detection, credential-stuffing protection, and compliance certifications (SOC 2, HIPAA BAA-eligible) that would otherwise have to be built and continuously proven in-house — the identical "don't build what a vendor already does well" logic ADR-002 used for Telehealth, applied here to identity.

**Trade-off:**
This means the private-cloud track — the option built specifically to keep everything under Meridian's own physical control — has one deliberate, external, internet-dependent SaaS dependency at its most sensitive trust boundary; a genuine tension worth stating openly rather than glossing over, since "private cloud" is sometimes assumed to mean total self-sufficiency and this decision says otherwise where the risk math doesn't support total self-sufficiency. If a fully self-hosted answer is ever mandated (a stricter future compliance requirement, or a network-isolation requirement ruling out any internet-facing SaaS dependency), Keycloak-on-Tanzu is the realistic fallback, accepting the full operational and security burden of running it, named here as a deferred option, not a dismissed one.
