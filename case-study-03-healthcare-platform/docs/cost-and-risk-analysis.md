# Cost and Risk Analysis — Meridian Health Network

Step 13, the final step of this case study's pipeline: a 3–5 year TCO comparison between the approved Azure migration (ADR-034 through ADR-038) and the "do nothing, stay and refresh" alternative, plus a consolidated risk register pulling together every risk named across Steps 1–12 rather than starting from a blank page. The full cost model, with live formulas, is [`finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx) — this document explains the model's structure, states the conclusion plainly, and carries the risk register.

## 1. Methodology and Honesty About Precision

Every dollar figure in this document and the workbook is an **illustrative, order-of-magnitude Azure retail (Pay-As-You-Go, East US) list-price approximation**, built for TCO *comparison* purposes — not a vendor quote, not a negotiated Enterprise Agreement or Microsoft Customer Agreement rate, and not a number Meridian's CFO should take to a budget committee without a real Azure pricing-calculator run and vendor engagement first. This is the same discipline every sizing ADR in this case study already applied to its own numbers (ADR-005's Citrix session density, ADR-006's SQL MI vCore count, ADR-010's Portal instance range are all explicitly "directional starting points... not measured") — Step 13 does the same thing for dollars that Steps 6–9 did for infrastructure sizing, and says so plainly rather than presenting invented precision as fact.

The comparison is baselined against `requirements.md`'s own instruction: evaluate the migration "against the existing capital-refresh baseline, not against $0." The on-prem "do-nothing" scenario below is not today's actual spend — it is what staying on-prem and refreshing the aging hardware `current-state.md` §3 already flags as due would cost, including the compliance-driven insurance penalty the current architecture cannot avoid without the redesign this whole case study is about.

Staffing (the 16-person internal team plus after-hours MSP NOC) is **excluded from the dollar comparison** as a wash: no scenario in this case study proposes changing headcount, so it doesn't differentiate the two paths on a pure-cost basis. It matters a great deal for *risk* instead — see Section 4's execution-capacity risk.

## 3. The Result — Stated Plainly

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 | Year 5 | Year 6 | Year 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Do-Nothing Baseline (annual) | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 | $1,070,400 |
| Azure Migration (annual) | $1,824,924 | $935,815 | $935,815 | $935,815 | $935,815 | $935,815 | $935,815 |
| **Cumulative Do-Nothing** | $1,070,400 | $2,140,800 | $3,211,200 | $4,281,600 | $5,352,000 | $6,422,400 | $7,492,800 |
| **Cumulative Azure Migration** | $1,824,924 | $2,760,739 | $3,696,554 | $4,632,370 | $5,568,185 | $6,504,000 | $7,439,815 |
| Cumulative delta (Azure − Do-Nothing) | +$754,524 | +$619,939 | +$485,354 | +$350,770 | +$216,185 | +$81,600 | **−$52,985** |

**This is not a "cloud is cheaper" result, and this document isn't going to pretend it is one.** Migrating costs more than doing nothing for every year of the requested 3–5 year window — Year 1 carries real one-time transition cost plus a genuine double-run period (legacy infrastructure stays fully operational per ADR-036 while the new Azure spend ramps up alongside it), and even by Year 5 the cumulative gap has only narrowed to about $216,000, not closed. The gap keeps narrowing every year — Reserved Capacity savings land in Year 2 (ADR-038), and the on-prem baseline carries a flat, un-shrinking insurance penalty every year it's held — and crosses over to favor Azure somewhere between Year 6 and Year 7. Extending the window that far is shown honestly as illustrative, past the 3–5 year horizon this analysis was actually asked to cover, not as a way to manufacture a win inside the requested window.

**The business case for this migration was never primarily a cost-savings case, and the numbers now confirm that rather than contradict it.** `problem-statement.md` §2 ranks the business drivers in this order: cyber-insurance/compliance mandate first, availability/resilience second, scalability for growth third, technical debt/end-of-support risk fourth, cost trajectory fifth. Cost was never the top argument — and this TCO model shows exactly why that ordering was right. The real, quantifiable wins are: RTO drops from 24–48+ hours to 180 minutes (`azure-implementation.md` §12); the insurance penalty this model prices at $360,000/year ($540,000 do-nothing vs. $180,000 migrated) is avoided starting in Wave 0, not after the full program; the capital refresh `requirements.md` says is "due... regardless" is avoided entirely, not deferred; and clinic onboarding drops from 4–6 months to weeks. A modest 5-year cost premium in exchange for those is a defensible trade, and this document says so directly instead of manufacturing a cost-savings story the numbers don't actually support.

## 4. What the Model Deliberately Doesn't Capture

- **No dollar value on downtime avoided.** A repeat of the January 2025 ice-storm outage (a full business day of paper scheduling across all 46 sites) has a real cost, but this model doesn't fabricate an expected-value figure for it — there's no real actuarial basis captured anywhere in this case study to make that number honest rather than invented. It's named here as a real, unpriced upside of the Azure design's faster RTO, not folded into the TCO table as a false-precision estimate.
- **No licensing-inventory offset.** The single largest line in the entire Azure run-rate is Entra ID P2 at $464,400/year (46% of steady-state spend) — `AzureRunRate` flags this directly: if Meridian already holds Microsoft 365 E5 or EMS E5 licensing for other reasons, this cost may already be substantially covered. That's a real licensing-inventory question this case study's inputs don't answer, not resolved here.
- **No vendor-negotiated discount.** Every rate is public list price. Real Azure spend at this scale (a multi-year, multi-hundred-thousand-dollar commitment) would realistically see a negotiated Enterprise Agreement discount on top of everything in this model — meaning the real-world Azure numbers are more likely to look better than this model shows, not worse, but that's a procurement outcome, not an architecture one.

## 6. Explicitly Deferred

- Actual Azure Enterprise Agreement / Microsoft Customer Agreement pricing negotiation — this model uses public list price throughout.
- A confirmed licensing inventory to resolve the Entra ID P2 overlap question named in Section 4 and the risk register.
- Direct confirmation with LabCorp, Quest, Surescripts, and the hospital PACS partners that their systems support the resend/reconciliation workflow every DR and rollback plan in this case study assumes.
- A direct conversation with Meridian's cyber insurer confirming that ADR-037's phased compliance approach will in fact be credited toward renewal terms ahead of full migration completion.
- Execution of the actual Reserved Instance / Savings Plan purchase described in ADR-038 — that happens after real Year 1 telemetry exists, not here.
