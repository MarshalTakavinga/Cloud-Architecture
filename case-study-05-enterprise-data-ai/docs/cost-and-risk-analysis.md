# Step 12: Cost, FinOps, and Risk Analysis (Final Step)

## Purpose

This step answers five questions:

- What does the [Step 10](target-architecture.md) GCP target cost over five years, including the [Step 11](migration-roadmap.md) program and the Teradata bridge?
- Does it meet driver 5 and NFR-11? That means a data-platform run-rate at or below the **$6.8M legacy baseline** (excluding AI), and an assistant unit cost of **≤ $0.05 per query** all-in.
- Does the claims assistant justify itself on its own unit economics, as driver 5 requires?
- Could cost evidence change the platform decision ([ADR-027](../adr/ADR-027-cloud-platform-selection.md))?
- What risks remain, consolidated across all 30 ADRs?

**Every figure here is illustrative.** The model is [`finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx), generated by [`finance/build_tco.py`](../finance/build_tco.py). It has 412 formulas and no errors after recalculation. Every hardcoded number is a blue input with its basis stated, and quote-based inputs are highlighted in yellow. The BigQuery edition slot prices are Google's September 2026 US list prices:

| BigQuery edition | Pay-as-you-go | 1-year commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | $0.06 per slot-hour | $0.054 |
| Enterprise Plus | $0.10 per slot-hour | $0.09 |

Everything else is a planning figure that must be replaced with a quote: Apigee, Vector Search sizing, model token prices, the Teradata hardware-support term, and loaded labor rates.

**Two cases are kept separate, because driver 5 separates them:**
- **A. Data platform:** a *cost* case against the status quo. The status quo means renewing Teradata, refreshing the appliance, and keeping SAS and Informatica.
- **B. Claims assistant:** a *value* case that must stand on its own unit economics.

### What the data-platform case rests on, stated plainly

- **The run-rate test passes by a wide margin.** The steady-state GCP data platform costs about **$1.83M a year** (Y3, services plus storage), which is **27% of the $6.8M baseline**. That is a saving of about **$4.8M a year**. It stays under the baseline even if BigQuery ran about **9,100 more average slots** than planned, or if the run-rate grew 3.7×. Two causes explain the gap:
  - Teradata's licence is priced per appliance, and it is most of the baseline.
  - The SAS grid's compute is replaced by serverless BigQuery and notebooks, with no licence.

  People, Tableau, and Guidewire are outside both sides of the comparison. **This is not a 73% cut in total data spending.**
- **The five-year case is thinner than the run-rate suggests**, because the exit costs about **$11M one-time**, plus the bridge. Payback on the data platform falls in **Year 4**, with a **five-year saving of about $6.9M**.
- **Most of that five-year saving is the avoided $6M appliance refresh.** If the status quo could somehow run five more years without a refresh, the five-year saving falls to **about $0.9M**, with payback in Y5. The steady-state saving of about $4.8M a year is unchanged, so the case keeps improving after Y5.
- **The bridge is expensive insurance, not waste.** The bridge year costs **$5.1M** (licence plus hardware support). About **$1.2M of that is premium** over the current rate. Exiting without it was rejected in [ADR-028](../adr/ADR-028-teradata-contract-and-exit-sequencing.md) as not credible. The alternative to paying this premium is an emergency renewal on Teradata's terms.
- **The target is not cheaper in Y1 or Y2.** Y2 is the most expensive year of the program, at about $13.6M against the status quo's $6.8M. Both estates run in parallel through May 2028, alongside most of the one-time spend. Finance should expect a **peak cumulative outlay of about $7.4M** above the status quo by the end of Y2.

### Data-platform sensitivity (recomputed in the model)

| Scenario | 5-yr saving | Payback |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | **$6.9M** | Y4 |
| Status quo without the appliance refresh | $0.9M | Y5 |

| Exit slips 3 months past the bridge (Teradata at the bridge rate, Informatica and hosting extended, dual-run longer) | $5.3M | Y4 |
| BigQuery compute +50% (all three reservations) | $4.7M | Y4 |
| One-time program +30% | $3.7M | Y5 |
| **Combined downside** (no refresh + 45% rewrite + 3-month slip + one-time +30%) | **−$5.2M** | after Y5 |

**Reading this:**
- **The rewrite estimate matters less than expected.** Each additional 5% of manual rewrite costs about $0.3M. It matters for the *schedule* (the bridge), not for the money.
- **The big levers are one-time overrun and schedule slip**, because both push spend into the years when both estates are still running.
- **The combined downside is negative over five years,** but it recovers within about one further year at $4.8M a year.

The **3-month slip** is the scenario that hurts operationally. Nothing in the current bridge terms covers it. That is why this step adds a condition to ADR-028 (see below).

## B. Claims Assistant: Unit Economics and Value

### Unit cost per query ([`AssistantUnitCost`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx) sheet)

| Component (per query unless stated) | $ |
|---|---|
| Routed generation: 70% fast model, 30% frontier model; 6,000 tokens in, 500 out | 0.0098 |
| Reranking, Model Armor screening, 5% online-evaluation sample, audit logging | 0.0031 |
| **Variable cost per query** | **0.0129** |
| Fixed AI infrastructure per year: Vector Search, Document AI OCR for about 25K new documents a day, embeddings, warm tier, **Apigee**, Cloud Run, evaluation harness, audit store | $539,750 |
| Fixed cost per query at full scale (27.5M queries a year: 4,400 users × 25 × 250 days) | 0.0196 |
| **All-in unit cost at scale (NFR-11 definition)** | **0.0325 — PASS against $0.05** |
| Run team per query (product owner, two engineers, golden-set SME share) | 0.0218 |
| Fully loaded, including the run team | 0.0544 |

**Findings:**

1. **The ceiling is met at scale, with about 35% headroom.** Break-even for the ceiling is about **14.6M queries a year**, or roughly **2,330 active users** (53% of the population). On the Step 11 ramp, that is reached around **August 2027 (M11)**.
2. **Context size decides the ceiling, not the model choice.**
   - Routing *everything* to the frontier model still passes, at **$0.048**.
   - Doubling the retrieved context to 12K tokens on the frontier model fails, at **$0.068**.

   This confirms [ADR-008](../adr/ADR-008-finops-allocation-and-unit-cost.md)'s levers, with a sharper order: **chunking and rerank quality ([ADR-006](../adr/ADR-006-retrieval-scope-and-chunking.md)) protect the budget more than model routing does.** The gateway alert should therefore track *tokens per query* as well as dollars.
3. **Fully loaded, the assistant costs about $0.054 a query, above the ceiling.** NFR-11 defines "all-in" as retrieval plus inference plus logging, so the requirement is met. The people cost is reported next to the unit cost anyway, so the headline figure doesn't hide it.
4. **Gate A2 fails by construction as written.** [ADR-029](../adr/ADR-029-decoupled-assistant-rollout.md) sets "cost per query ≤ $0.05" as a *pilot* gate. With 300 users (about 1.9M queries a year), even pilot-sized fixed costs give about **$0.157 a query**, whatever the design quality. As written, the gate would block scale for the wrong reason. **Amended in the ADR-029 addendum:**
   - A2 tests the **measured variable cost per query ≤ $0.02** (the base case is $0.013).
   - A2 also tests the **modeled at-scale all-in cost ≤ $0.05**, using the pilot's *measured* tokens per query.
   - The monthly all-in ≤ $0.05 test applies from **M14**, when all users are on.

**Value** = claims users × $95K loaded cost × 30% of time spent searching × search-time reduction (15% in Y1, 25% from Y2) × 50% realization.

**What the value case rests on, stated plainly:**
- **It is capacity, not cash,** unless Harborline converts it. The 50% realization assumes the released time avoids hiring, overtime, and independent-adjuster spend as claim volumes grow. The rest shows up as quality and cycle time, which drive the LAE goal in driver 2 but are not counted here.
- **The 25% search-time reduction is an assumption.** No target is stated in `requirements.md`, and gate A2 measures the real figure in the pilot.
- **The case survives very large misses.** The assistant breaks even at a **2.8% cut in search time**, about **0.8% of an adjuster's working day** (roughly four minutes). With both the reduction and the realization halved (12.5% and 25%), steady-state value is still **about 2.3× cost**.
- **The value no model can price** is replacing shadow AI. The March 2026 audit found medical details pasted into a public chatbot. The cost of a single regulatory action or breach notification under the NAIC bulletin regime isn't modeled. That exposure is the real reason driver 1 ranks first.

## Can Cost Evidence Change the Platform Decision?

**Not on its own.** GCP leads Azure by 0.225 weighted points, and cost carries a 15% weight. GCP would have to lose **1.5 points** on the cost criterion (from 3.5 to 2.0), with Azure unchanged, before the decision flips (see the [`NFR11Tests`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx) sheet). Nothing in this model suggests a gap of that size.

- The GCP-specific costs are modest: Apigee ($180K) and Enterprise Plus for managed DR (about $240K).
- The large costs apply to every track: the Teradata exit labor, the bridge, and SAS porting.

This differs from Case Study 4, where the edge licence stack was the dominant platform-specific cost and the review trigger could flip the decision. **Here the decision rests on gate G0** (Iceberg parity and model terms), **not on price.** Quotes are still obtained at G0 for Apigee, Vector Search, and model pricing, to replace the yellow inputs.

## Changes Made to Earlier Decisions in This Step

- **[ADR-029](../adr/ADR-029-decoupled-assistant-rollout.md) addendum:** gate A2's cost criterion is replaced. The same change is made to the gate table in [`migration-roadmap.md`](migration-roadmap.md).
- **[ADR-028](../adr/ADR-028-teradata-contract-and-exit-sequencing.md) addendum:** a **month-to-month extension option at the bridge rate** is added to the bridge negotiation, to cover risk 1.
