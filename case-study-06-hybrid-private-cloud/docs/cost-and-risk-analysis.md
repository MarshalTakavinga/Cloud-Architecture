# Step 13: Cost and Risk Analysis (Final Step)

## Purpose

This step answers five questions:

- What does the [Step 11](target-architecture.md) Azure Local + Arc target cost over five years, including the [Step 12](migration-roadmap.md) bridge and migration?
- Does it meet **NFR-10**: five-year platform TCO at or below the status-quo path (VCF renewal + like-for-like refresh)?
- What Broadcom price would reverse [ADR-031](../adr/ADR-031-platform-selection.md) at **gate G0-2**?
- Which assumptions does the case rest on?
- What risks remain, consolidated across all 34 ADRs?

**Every figure here is illustrative.** The model is [`finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx), generated by [`finance/build_tco.py`](../finance/build_tco.py). It has 446 formulas and no errors after recalculation. Every hardcoded number is a blue input with its basis stated, and quote-based inputs are yellow. The Azure Local host fee ($10 per core per month, L1, waived by Azure Hybrid Benefit) is Microsoft's published pricing. Broadcom, Omnissa, AVD, and hardware prices are planning figures to be replaced with quotes at G0.

**Three paths are compared:**
- **Status quo (NFR-10's baseline):** renew VCF at the 3-year quote and refresh like for like.
- **VCF track (Step 6):** renewal + vDefend + Live Recovery + vSAN ESA refresh + enablement.
- **Azure Local + Arc (selected):** a 15-month VCF bridge at a +10% premium, then Azure Local.

**Common lines appear in all three and cancel in every comparison:** Windows Server SA, RHEL, Veeam, facilities, and the $1.2M MRA resilience program, which is mandatory whatever the platform. The **new governance and landing-zone capabilities** (about $0.74M a year) appear in both the VCF and Azure tracks but not the status quo. They are excluded from the NFR-10 test, as Case Study 5 excluded the AI capability from its baseline test.

## What the Cost Case Rests On, Stated Plainly

- **NFR-10 passes, narrowly.** The Azure platform TCO over five years is **$30.56M against $31.87M**, a margin of **$1.31M (4%)**. The five-year window is the pessimistic view, because it holds two years of dual running.
- **The steady state is the real case.** From Y3, the like-for-like run-rate is **about $1.73M a year lower** than the status quo ($3.71M against $5.45M). The difference is:
  - no Broadcom subscription
  - no Omnissa
  - host fees waived
  - less legacy maintenance

  After paying for the new governance and landing zone, the saving is still about **$1.0M a year**.
- **The VCF track is the most expensive path**, at $38.2M. It keeps the full Broadcom subscription *and* adds vDefend and Live Recovery to fix what the status quo leaves broken. **Fixing the MRA on VMware costs more than leaving VMware.**
- **Y1–Y2 are the expensive years.** The peak cumulative outlay above the status quo is about **$3.5M at the end of Y2**. Payback on the NFR-10 comparison comes in **Y5**, and it keeps improving after that.
- **Two assumptions carry the case**, and neither is a price. The sensitivity below shows it:
  1. **Azure Hybrid Benefit covers every host core.** At 0% coverage, the host fees would add **$2.05M** and **NFR-10 would fail by $0.73M**. At 50% coverage, it passes by $0.29M.
  2. **The 32 hosts bought in 2023 can be re-imaged into Azure Local** (they must be on the validated catalog). If they can't, replacing them adds about $1.6M and **NFR-10 fails by $0.29M**.

  **Both are therefore added to gate G0** (see the ADR-031 addendum). They are licensing and hardware-catalog confirmations the bank can get in writing before M4.

## NFR-10 and Gate Sensitivity (recomputed in the model)

| Scenario | NFR-10 margin (5-yr) | G0-2 threshold (discount from quote) |
|---|---|---|
| **Base** | **+$1.31M** (pass) | **$0.89M/yr (−46%)** |
| Bridge premium 25% (not 10%) | +$1.00M | −45% |
| Bridge runs 12 + 3 months at +25% | +$0.49M | −44% |
| AZ one-time program +30% | +$0.47M | −37% |
| No uplift at the 2030 VMware renewal | +$0.94M | −45% |
| Azure Hybrid Benefit covers only 50% | +$0.29M | −35% |
| **2023 hosts can't be reused** (+$1.6M capex) | **−$0.29M (fail)** | −29% |
| Azure Hybrid Benefit covers 0% | **−$0.73M (fail)** | n/a |
| **Combined downside** (bridge 12 + 3 at +25%, no reuse, one-time +30%) | **−$1.96M (fail)** | **−12.5%** |

**Reading this:**
- **Bridge and program overruns erode the margin but don't break it.**
- **The two Microsoft-side assumptions can break it.** They are verifiable, which is why they belong in G0.
- **The G0-2 threshold is robust in the base case.** Broadcom would need to cut its quote by **46%** for VCF to win, against the ~22% average reduction reported for renewals negotiated with a costed exit alternative ([Redress Compliance](https://redresscompliance.com/vcf-licensing-guide-2026)).
- **But in the combined downside, a 12.5% Broadcom discount would be enough.** The threshold must be recomputed at G0 with the real Microsoft-side answers, **not frozen now**.

## Gate G0-2: The Broadcom Threshold Price ([`ThresholdTest`](../finance/TCO-Analysis.xlsx) sheet)

Step 10's sensitivity analysis showed that ADR-031 flips to VCF if VCF's cost score rises from 2.0 to **3.5**. Mapping cost scores linearly onto five-year TCO (2.0 at VCF's quote, 4.5 at Azure's), VCF must close **60% of the $4.25M five-year gap** between the tracks.

Both tracks depend on the Broadcom price:

| Track | Sensitivity to the annual price | Why |
|---|---|---|
| VCF | **4.725 years-equivalent** | Renewal months plus the uplifted months after 2030 |
| Azure | **1.375** | The 15-month bridge at +10% |

| | Annual VCF price | Discount from the $1.65M quote |
|---|---|---|
| **Threshold (decision flips)** | **≈ $0.89M** | **−46%** |
| Parity (five-year TCO equal) | ≈ $0.38M | −77% |

**Result:** on illustrative pricing, **a Broadcom offer below about $0.89M a year for three years reverses ADR-031 to VCF.** Anything above it confirms Azure Local. The bank goes into the negotiation **with a tested, costed alternative** (the G0 proof of concept). That is exactly the leverage driver 2 was written to create. If Broadcom meets the threshold, the bank has won the negotiation. If it doesn't, the bank has its answer.

## The Oracle Lever (platform-neutral, not in the path totals)

Consolidating the Oracle clusters from 640 to 512 licensed cores at refresh would reduce processor licences from **320 to 256**. That is a potential **$0.67M a year** in Oracle support at illustrative rates, **larger than the steady-state saving from the hypervisor decision.**

It is shown separately because:
1. it is available on **every** path
2. it is realizable only if the licences are **terminated**, and Oracle's repricing policy often claws back much of the saving on the remaining support

It is worth a specialist review before the refresh order, whichever platform wins ([ADR-007](../adr/ADR-007-oracle-cluster-and-licence-boundary.md)).
